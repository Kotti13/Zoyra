ZOYRA STORIES · LOVE

Songs we couldn't say out loud.

On love, courage, and the three minutes and forty seconds it takes to tell someone how you feel without saying a word.

By the Zoyra team · 6 min read

There is a particular kind of silence that happens right before you send someone a song. Your thumb hovers. You read the title again and wonder if it says too much, or not enough. You think about deleting it. Then you press send, and for a moment the whole world is just a grey tick waiting to turn into two.

People have always done this. Before phones, they made mixtapes and wrote the track list by hand. Before tapes, they asked the band to play one more slow one. Music has been our way of saying the thing underneath the thing for as long as there's been music.

Love is mostly small, repeated things

The movies get love wrong in one important way: they make it loud. Real love is quieter. It's remembering which song they always skip. It's the playlist named after an inside joke. It's sending a track at 1 a.m. with no message at all, because they'll know.

When we started building Zoyra, we kept coming back to that. We didn't want another app that measured how many people were watching you. We wanted one that noticed who you kept coming back to.

"This one made me think of the rooftop. You know the one." "Play it at 2:14. That's the part. That's us." "No reason. Just wanted you to have it today."

Courage, three minutes at a time

A dedication is a small act of bravery. On Zoyra you can send a song with a message attached, or drop a single lyric line into a chat, and let the music carry the part you can't quite say. Some people use it to apologise. Some use it to say "I miss you." Some use it to start something.

And some people send a time capsule: a message that waits, sealed, until a date you choose. A birthday. An anniversary. The first morning after a long distance finally closes.

"The playlist is the love letter. The shuffle button is the hope."

Remembering together

Love is also memory. Zoyra's On This Day shows you the song you played most on today's date in earlier years, and sometimes that song is a whole person. Blend takes your listening and someone else's last 90 days and turns the overlap into one playlist, a map of where your tastes meet.

We think the best feeling an app can give you is the one where you look up from your phone and want to call somebody.

And then, forever

For the person who's been there the longest, we built Forever. You ask, they accept, and a gold ring joins your profiles. A streak counts the days you've shared music. A private vault keeps the songs that marked the chapters. It can't be undone with a block, which is exactly why both of you have to choose it.

It isn't only for couples. Forever is for the best friend who knew you before you knew yourself, the sibling who sends memes at 3 a.m., the parent who just learned how to share a song. Love comes in a lot of shapes. So does forever.

So here's our small dare: think of the person who came to mind while you read this. Find the song. Don't overthink the message. Press send.
